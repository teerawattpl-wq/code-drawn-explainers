# The short work order

One page per story, written after the topic is chosen and the facts are checked, and shown to
the user before any narration or code. It replaces the plain script table of step 2.

## Why

A first trial used a long work order (concept, colours as hex codes, fonts, camera, rhythm,
half-second storyboard, numbers for every size). Every scene's stills passed on the first
look, and the banned list really prevented mistakes. But the page was about 150 lines, and
the times written in it were wrong anyway, because real timing only exists once the
narration is recorded: the film came out ten seconds longer than planned. The user kept the
work order and asked for it short. Colours, type, camera and rhythm are the maker's job.

## The three parts

**1. Concept and the continuity rule.** One sentence that every shot must serve, and one rule
for how a shot comes out of the one before. Examples of rules that worked: "the thing that
swings in one scene shrinks into the thing that swings in the next"; "one sheet of paper is on
screen in every scene, and every scene comes from folding or unfolding it"; "the pen at the end
of the chain never leaves the screen".

**2. The banned list.** Six to eight lines, specific to this story. Write what would be wrong,
misleading or lazy here, for example:

- a picture that repeats a popular misconception (the atom drawn as planets on orbits)
- a claim the sources do not support ("the most accurate in the world" for a new device)
- formulas on screen, when the audience is not ready for them
- real brands, logos or a reference post's own pictures
- more than six words of text on screen at once; more than two arrows in a scene
- a scene in which the story's subject does not change

**3. Narration and the drawing list.** For each line: the narration text, then what will be
drawn - background, main subject, motion, supporting detail, diagram - in a few short bullets.
Numbers only where they are content (a count, an angle, a year). No times: they come from the
recording.

Close with a table of facts: each fact, its source, and how sure you are (read at the source,
calculated, or taken from a summary only). Leave out what sources disagree on, and say so.

## After approval

Record the narration (step 3). Everything after that follows the normal workflow. Add each
decision and each fix to the end of the work order as the story goes on, so the file is the
story's history.
